{{ _('Where does the choice come from?') }}

{{ _('When you click “Choose for me,” the server submits a one-qubit circuit to operational IBM Quantum hardware. A Hadamard gate puts the qubit into an equal superposition, then the circuit measures it once. The measured bit selects right or left. This uses IBM Quantum hardware rather than a local simulator; the result is not persisted.') }}

{{ _('The random result is only used to select the app\'s suggestion. It does not make your decision for you: you remain free to follow the suggestion, choose the other way, or do neither.') }}

{{ _('What is a “quantum fluctuation”?') }}

{{ _('In physics, the term usually describes quantum variations in physical quantities, such as fields, which play a role in theories and experimentally studied phenomena. This app\'s random bit comes from measuring a qubit on IBM Quantum hardware; it does not directly measure a fluctuation in your body, mind, or surroundings.') }}

{{ _('What about the many-worlds interpretation?') }}

{{ _('The many-worlds interpretation is one interpretation of quantum mechanics. In simplified terms, it describes quantum evolution as branching states that stop interfering with one another through decoherence. It is a theoretical interpretation; there is no consensus that it is the correct description of reality, and its branches are not worlds we can visit or send messages between.') }}

{{ _('People sometimes use the analogy, “in one branch I chose right; in another, left.” This image may help illustrate the interpretation, but it does not show that your consciousness or presence is copied, travels, or splits. This page cannot identify, create, or observe versions of you in parallel universes.') }}
